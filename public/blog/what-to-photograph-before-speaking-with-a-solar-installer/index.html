What to photograph before speaking with a solar installer

Your electricity bill explains your energy use. A few useful photographs help clarify the physical starting point.

Watts Better · September 2026

Your electricity bill explains your energy use. Photographs explain the physical starting point.

Neither replaces a site assessment, and neither is meant to. Together they turn a vague first conversation into a specific one.

Why photographs help

Most first conversations stall on the same handful of questions — what does the switchboard look like, where could equipment actually go, how is the roof accessed.

A few photographs answer those questions before anyone drives out. That means the conversation starts at the things that actually need deciding.

The switchboard

  • A straight-on photograph of the board with its cover closed.
  • A closer shot of any labels or ratings printed on the outside.
  • Enough distance in the frame to show the wall around it and how much space there is.
SAFETY FIRST

Do not open the switchboard cover, remove any panel or touch wiring to take a photograph. Photograph what is already exposed, from outside, and leave the enclosure closed.

The roof and its access

  • A wide shot of the roof, taken from the ground — skip this one if any part of it is unsafe to reach.
  • The route an installer would use to get up there.
  • Anything already on the roof: existing panels, an antenna, a vent, a skylight.

Where equipment might go

  • The wall or area where an inverter or battery could be mounted.
  • That same spot photographed at a different time of day, if it is shaded part of the day.
  • The distance between that spot and the switchboard.

The details people forget

  • The electricity meter and its enclosure.
  • Anything that limits access — locking gates, a narrow side path, a steep driveway.
  • Existing damage you already know about: cracked tiles, rust, water staining. Photographing it first avoids an argument later about when it happened.

What photographs cannot tell an installer

  • The condition of the roof structure beneath the surface.
  • Cable routes and whether there is spare capacity in the switchboard.
  • Whether equipment will actually fit once clearance requirements are applied.
  • Anything a physical site assessment is designed to confirm.

This is why the photographs replace an initial conversation, not an inspection. Treat them as the starting point of an assessment, not a substitute for one.

Your photo checklist

  • Switchboard, cover closed, straight on.
  • Switchboard labels, close up.
  • Wall or area where equipment might be mounted.
  • The same spot in shade, if it is shaded part of the day.
  • Roof, wide shot from the ground.
  • Roof access route.
  • Meter and its enclosure.
  • Anything you already know is damaged.
  • Anything that limits site access.

Frequently asked questions

01Why do photographs help before speaking with an installer?

Your bill explains your energy use. Photographs clarify the physical starting point, so the conversation begins from an accurate picture of your home rather than a description of it.

02What should I photograph?

The things an installer would ask about: the switchboard, the roof and its access, the intended equipment location, and anything that might restrict the installation.

03Should I open the switchboard to photograph inside it?

No. Never open the enclosure or touch wiring to take a photograph. Photograph the closed board and its external labelling only.

04Do photographs replace a site assessment?

No. They help the conversation start from an accurate picture, but a site assessment is still required before any design is confirmed.
